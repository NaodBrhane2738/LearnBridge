Accessible Media & Learning Delivery

Understanding how LearnBridge provides decoupled, universal accessibility infrastructure to existing LMS platforms like ALX Savanna.

Ethiopian Sign Language (ETSL)

Why External LMS Integration Matters

In traditional online learning, accessibility is often implemented as an afterthought—or locked inside a vendor's proprietary media player. If a learner requires bilingual Amharic and English captions, high-contrast text, or Ethiopian Sign Language interpretations, they are usually forced to use cumbersome third-party screen overlays or miss out entirely.

Key Architectural Principle: LearnBridge operates as an API-First Accessibility Layer. The ALX Savanna LMS does not need to store medical diagnoses or invent assistive tools; it simply queries GET /api/v1/accessibility/preferences using the student's authenticated session, and receives the exact visual, auditory, and motor preferences to apply.

Multi-Modal Educational Formats

Under WCAG 2.2 Level AA requirements, all synchronous and asynchronous learning media must provide equivalent alternatives:

  • Captions: Synchronized text for learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing, available in Ethiopian national languages.
  • Audio Description: Spoken narration of critical visual charts, diagrams, and physical gestures.
  • Ethiopian Sign Language (ETSL): Native sign interpretation delivered via low-bandwidth video viewports.
  • Cognitive Simplification: Clean, distraction-free typographic hierarchy with adjustable line spacing.

Knowledge Check

How does LearnBridge protect learner privacy when communicating with third-party LMS platforms?

Answer:

LearnBridge stores strictly functional preferences (e.g. highContrast: true, captionsEnabled: true), never clinical diagnoses or disability labels. Third-party platforms receive only the styling and behavioral tokens needed to render an accessible experience.
